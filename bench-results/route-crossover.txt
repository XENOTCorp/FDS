route matcher: linear scan vs deterministic-automaton (trie) crossover
=====================================================================
Date: 2026-08-25
Repo: Atomos (src/kernel/rules.rs), thesis NT58/NT59/NT65

Method
------
Synthetic rule sets: R rules {GET /r0/*, ..., GET /r(R-1)/*}, matched
against the fixed path /r17/a/b/c/d/e/f/g/h (O(L), L=16) with
std::hint::black_box accumulation, 200k iterations per cell.
Same rule set, same paths, both realizations: match_linear (scan) vs
match_method (automaton). Automaton forced via Ruleset::from_rules_forced.

Results (release profile, nanoseconds per match, lower is better)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
   R    scan ns    trie ns  winner
   2        5.9       29.4  scan
   4       10.6       31.7  scan
   8       25.8       31.8  scan
  16       59.6       43.9  trie
  24       97.9       70.0  trie

  40       79.7       69.7  trie
  48       78.6       70.6  trie
  56       75.8       78.4  scan
  63       72.3       70.5  trie

Interpretation (thesis NT58, affine crossover)
----------------------------------------------
Scan cost is affine in R: fitted c_scan(R) ~= 4.2*R - 2.5 ns
(5.9 @ R=2, 25.8 @ R=8, 59.6 @ R=16). The automaton is flat in R
(~35-44 ns, noise-dominated). Fitted crossover R* ~= 10-12;
on the measured grid {2,4,8,16,24,...} the automaton first wins at
R=16 (44 vs 60 ns) -> TRIE_MIN_RULES = 16 (lattice minimum, NT59).

Production consequence
----------------------
The shipped configs (templates/rules.json, examples/*) have R <= 8:
the crossover theorem says the linear scan is the cheaper realization
there, so those configs never build the automaton (trie_active()=false)
and the cached/miss dispatch numbers are bit-for-bit unchanged.

Correctness
-----------
200k-path fuzz (automaton vs scan) in kernel::rules::tests: identical
results on all paths, including prefix-boundary, method-split, and
exclude-veto cases. 13/13 rules tests pass.
