\chapter{Kleisli Adjunction and Pure/Effectful Decomposition}\label{ch:kleisli}

\paragraph{Two regimes, one category.}
The subcategories of \autoref{ch:mol} divide molecules by what they carry.
A \emph{pure} molecule has unit state space, hence is a total function
between types, and $\mathbf{PureMol}$ is equivalent to the category of
types and total functions (\autoref{thm:4}). An \emph{effectful} molecule
over a context type $\mathit{Ctx}$ has state space $\mathit{Ctx}$, and
$\mathbf{EffMol} (\mathit{Ctx})$ is isomorphic to the Kleisli category of
the state monad on $\mathit{Ctx}$ (\autoref{thm:5},
\autoref{sec:prelim-monads}). A \emph{hybrid} molecule is any molecule of
Mol: an arbitrary state space with an arbitrary transition.

Dataplane stages are hybrids: a parser keeps a position, a checksummer an
accumulator, a transport sequence numbers, and around each piece of state
there is pure work (masking, scaling, reformatting) that must not
itself hold state. This chapter proves that the two regimes coexist
canonically: every hybrid molecule $M$ factors, uniquely up to
isomorphism, as $M = q \circ e \circ p$ with $p$ and $q$ pure and $e$
effectful (\autoref{thm:33}); the effectful core carries exactly the state
and the pure wings carry exactly the stateless work. This is the Kleisli
decomposition of Mol: every morphism is a Kleisli morphism of the state
monad (\autoref{sec:prelim-monads}) read between two pure maps.

\paragraph{Loops and hoisting.}
Mol is a traced monoidal category: the trace $\mathrm{Tr}^{U}_{A,B}$,
constructed in \autoref{ch:trace}, feeds the $U$-output of a loop body back
into its $U$-input and satisfies the axioms of \autoref{sec:prelim-trace}
\citep{joyalsv}. The trace laws: sliding (\autoref{thm:34}), vanishing
and yanking (\autoref{thm:35}), superposing (\autoref{thm:36}): govern
where computation may live relative to a feedback loop, and together with
the decomposition theorem they license \emph{hoisting}: pure work is moved
out of loops, and loop state that is purely determined by the rest of the
state is compressed away. Hoisting shrinks the loop-state footprint, and
the cache footprint of loop state is monotone in the size of that state
(\autoref{thm:37}). These are the theorems behind the engineering rule :
state in one place, pure work outside the loop: that
\autoref{ch:coalgebra} quantifies by minimization and \autoref{ch:trace}
by iteration bounds, and that the software standard turns into policy.

\begin{theorem}[Kleisli decomposition (\autoref{thm:33})]\label{thm:33}
Every hybrid molecule $M : A \to B$ decomposes as $M = q \circ e \circ p$
with $p, q$ pure and $e$ effectful, uniquely up to isomorphism. Given a
Mealy presentation $(S, s_0, \mathrm{step})$ of $M$, with
$\mathrm{step} : S \times A \to B \times S$ \citep{mealy}, the canonical
factorization is
\[
p : A \to A \otimes S,\quad p (a) = (a, s_0),
\qquad
q : B \otimes S \to B,\quad q (b, s) = b ,
\]
and $e : A \otimes S \to B \otimes S$ is the effectful molecule with state
space $S$ and initial state $s_0$ whose transition runs one step of $M$
from its own state and writes the successor state to its output wire:
\[
\delta_e\bigl (s, (a, s')\bigr) = \bigl ((b, s''), s''\bigr),
\qquad (b, s'') = \mathrm{step} (s, a) .
\]
Uniqueness is twofold. \emph{(i)} The effectful core is invariant: if
$M = q' \circ e' \circ p'$ is any factorization with $p', q'$ pure and
$e'$ effectful with state space $S'$, then $S' \cong S$. \emph{(ii)} The
canonical triple is well defined on presentations: if $(S_1, s_1,
\mathrm{step}_1)$ and $(S_2, s_2, \mathrm{step}_2)$ present the same
morphism through the state bijection $\varphi$, then $p_2 = (1_A \otimes
\varphi) \circ p_1$, $q_1 = q_2 \circ (1_B \otimes \varphi)$, and $e_2$ is
the $\varphi$-conjugate of $e_1$; hence the decomposition is unique up to
isomorphism.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Existence.} Compose the three machines. The composite
$q \circ e \circ p$ has state space $() \otimes S \otimes ()$, initial
state $s_0$ up to the canonical identification, and transition: on input
$a$, $p$ emits $(a, s_0)$; $e$ in state $s$ computes
$(b, s'') = \mathrm{step} (s, a)$, emits $(b, s'')$, and advances to
$s''$; $q$ projects to $b$. Hence the composite transitions
$(s, a) \mapsto (b, s'')$ with $(b, s'') = \mathrm{step} (s, a)$, which is
exactly the transition of the presentation $(S, s_0, \mathrm{step})$
(\autoref{thm:1}): the machines are equal in Mol, so $M = q \circ e \circ p$.

\emph{(i).} In Mol, equality of morphisms is equality up to state-space
bijection (\autoref{thm:2}). The composite $q' \circ e' \circ p'$ has state
space $() \otimes S' \otimes () \cong S'$ (\autoref{thm:1}); since it equals
$M$, presented by the state space $S$, the two state spaces are
isomorphic: $S' \cong S$.

\emph{(ii).} The bijection $\varphi$ satisfies $\varphi (s_1) = s_2$ and
$\mathrm{step}_2 (\varphi (s), a) = (b, \varphi (s'))$ whenever
$(b, s') = \mathrm{step}_1 (s, a)$. Then
$p_2 (a) = (a, s_2) = (a, \varphi (s_1)) = (1_A \otimes \varphi)(p_1 (a))$,
and $q_2 \circ (1_B \otimes \varphi)(b, s) = q_2 (b, \varphi (s)) = b =
q_1 (b, s)$. For $e$, writing $\delta_e^i$ for the transition of $e_i$,
\[
\delta_e^2\bigl (\varphi (s), (a, s')\bigr)
= \bigl ((b, \varphi (s'')), \varphi (s'')\bigr),
\qquad (b, s'') = \mathrm{step}_1 (s, a) ,
\]
which is exactly the $\varphi$-conjugate of $\delta_e^1$: the machines
$e_1$ and $e_2$ are the same morphism of Mol (\autoref{thm:2}), and the
canonical triples are carried to each other by $\varphi$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sliding (\autoref{thm:34})]\label{thm:34}
Let $M : A \otimes U \to B \otimes U$ be the body of a feedback loop and let
$f : B \to B'$ be pure. Then
\[
\mathrm{Tr}^{U}_{A,B'}\bigl ((f \otimes 1_U) \circ M\bigr)
= f \circ \mathrm{Tr}^{U}_{A,B} (M) ,
\]
written in the catalog as $\mathrm{Tr} (f;M) = f;\mathrm{Tr} (M)$: a pure
computation applied inside every iteration may be slid out of the loop and
applied exactly once after it \citep{joyalsv}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The trace of \autoref{ch:trace} makes Mol a traced monoidal category in the
sense of \autoref{sec:prelim-trace}, whose axioms are the laws of
\citet{joyalsv}; in particular the trace is natural in the output object
$B$: for every morphism $g : B \to B'$,
$\mathrm{Tr}^{U}_{A,B'} ((g \otimes 1_U) \circ M) = g \circ
\mathrm{Tr}^{U}_{A,B} (M)$. Instantiating $g = f$, which is pure, gives the
equation. The machine-level content is direct: the feedback register
evolves by the loop body's transition alone, and $f \otimes 1_U$ touches
only the feed-forward output factor, so the register trajectory is
unchanged whether $f$ runs inside every iteration or once after the loop;
the accumulated effect is the single application of $f$ to the loop's
output.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Vanishing and yanking (\autoref{thm:35})]\label{thm:35}
The trace on Mol satisfies the vanishing and yanking laws of
\citet{joyalsv}:
\[
\mathrm{Tr}^{I}_{A,B} (M) = M ,
\qquad
\mathrm{Tr}^{U \otimes U}_{A,B} (M)
= \mathrm{Tr}^{U}_{A,B}\Bigl (
  \mathrm{Tr}^{U}_{A \otimes U,\, B \otimes U} (M) \Bigr),
\]
the second written in the catalog as $\mathrm{Tr} (\mathrm{Tr} (M)) =
\mathrm{Tr} (M)$: tracing over a doubled feedback register is iterating the
trace; and
\[
\mathrm{Tr}^{U}_{U,U} (\sigma_{U,U}) = 1_U ,
\]
where $\sigma_{U,U} : U \otimes U \to U \otimes U$ is the symmetry
(\autoref{sec:prelim-mon}): a feedback loop on a swap is the identity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
These are axioms of a traced monoidal category (\autoref{sec:prelim-trace}),
and the trace on Mol satisfies them by construction (\autoref{ch:trace}).
The machine-level readings are immediate: tracing over the unit object adds
no register, so the machine is unchanged; tracing over $U \otimes U$ adds
two coupled registers, which is the same as adding one register and feeding
it back once more; and $\mathrm{Tr} (\sigma)$ closes the loop on the pure
swap, which absorbs the input into the register and returns it on the next
step, so on stabilized loop values the loop is the identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Superposing (\autoref{thm:36})]\label{thm:36}
Let $M : A \otimes U \to B \otimes U$ and let $N : C \to D$ be any molecule.
Then
\[
\mathrm{Tr}^{U}_{A \otimes C,\, B \otimes D} (M \otimes N)
= \mathrm{Tr}^{U}_{A,B} (M) \otimes N ,
\]
written in the catalog as $\mathrm{Tr} (M) \otimes N = \mathrm{Tr} (M \otimes
N)$: a feedback loop running beside an independent molecule is the loop
and the molecule side by side.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is the superposing axiom of a traced monoidal category
(\autoref{sec:prelim-trace}), satisfied by Mol's trace by construction
(\autoref{ch:trace}). At the machine level, tensoring $N$ beside $M$
places the state space of $N$ beside that of $M$, and the feedback register
couples only to the $M$-factor, so closing the loop leaves the dynamics of
$N$ untouched; the state space of the loop is $S_M \otimes U \otimes S_N$,
which is exactly the tensor of $\mathrm{Tr} (M)$ with $N$
(\autoref{thm:3}).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hoisting and loop-state footprint (\autoref{thm:37})]\label{thm:37}
Let $M : A \otimes U \to B \otimes U$ be the body of a feedback loop,
presented by a machine $(S, \mathrm{step})$, and suppose the loop state
carries purely redundant information: $U = U' \otimes U''$ with a pure map
$h : U' \to U''$ such that the register is initialized in the graph
$i = \langle 1_{U'}, h\rangle : U' \to U$ and every register value in the
run lies in that graph, and the loop body preserves the graph:
$M \circ (1_A \otimes i) = (1_B \otimes i) \circ M'$ for the compressed
body $M' : A \otimes U' \to B \otimes U'$. Then:
\emph{(a) Hoisting.} $\mathrm{Tr}^{U} (M) \simeq \mathrm{Tr}^{U'} (M')$
(\autoref{thm:6}); the pure redundancy $h$ is hoisted out of the loop, and
the loop-state footprint shrinks from $|U| = |U'| \cdot |U''|$ to $|U'|$.
\emph{(b) Monotonicity.} With fixed-size state records of $r$ bytes, the
resident cache footprint of a loop with loop state $W$ is $|W| \cdot r$
bytes, strictly increasing in $|W|$; hence the cache footprint of loop
state is monotone in state size, and the hoisted loop of (a) is strictly
smaller whenever $|U''| > 1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{(a).} The loop $\mathrm{Tr}^{U} (M)$ carries state space $S \otimes
U$; its register is initialized in the graph, and since the body preserves
the graph, the register stays in the graph at every step, so all reachable
states lie in $S \otimes i (U')$. Conjugate by the bijection $\varphi :
S \otimes i (U') \to S \otimes U'$, $\varphi (s, (u', h (u'))) = (s, u')$.
On a reachable state with input $a$ the full loop computes
$M\bigl (a, (u', h (u'))\bigr) = (1_B \otimes i)\bigl (M'(a, u')\bigr)$, so it
emits the same output and advances to $(s', (u'_{1}, h (u'_{1})))$, whose
image under $\varphi$ is $(s', u'_{1})$; the compressed loop
$\mathrm{Tr}^{U'} (M')$ on $(s, u')$ with input $a$ emits the same output
and advances to $(s', u'_{1})$: the register evolves by the loop body's
transition in both loops (\autoref{ch:trace}). The two machines are
therefore behaviorally equivalent, $\mathrm{Tr}^{U} (M) \simeq
\mathrm{Tr}^{U'} (M')$ (\autoref{thm:6}), and their minimal realizations
(\autoref{ch:coalgebra}) are isomorphic. The compressed loop stores only
$u'$; the value $u'' = h (u')$ is recomputed by the pure map $h$ where it
is consumed, outside the loop, so the resident loop state shrinks from
$|U'| \cdot |U''|$ to $|U'|$ elements.

\emph{(b).} Fixed-size records of $r$ bytes make the resident working set
of a loop with loop state $W$ exactly $|W| \cdot r$ bytes, a strictly
increasing function of the state cardinality $|W|$. Replacing $U$ by $U'$
as in (a) multiplies the footprint by the factor $1/|U''| \leq 1$, and
strictly so whenever $|U''| > 1$: the cache footprint of loop state is
monotone in state size.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Hoisting in practice.}
Together the theorems give the operational shape of a hot loop. By
\autoref{thm:33} a hybrid stage $M = q \circ e \circ p$ runs as pure
initiation $p$, the effectful core $e$, pure completion $q$; the state of
the stage is exactly the state of $e$, and pure work holds none. By
\autoref{thm:34}--\autoref{thm:36} any feedback loop over such a stage can be
rearranged so that pure work and independent side computations live outside
the loop, and by \autoref{thm:37} loop state that is purely determined by
the remainder of the loop state is compressed away. The loop that runs on
the hot path is therefore the one with the smallest resident state: the
state of $e$ alone, after compression. This is what the software standard's
policies [ALLOC] and [CACHE] enforce: no allocation in the hot path, the
loop state small enough to stay resident: and what the following
chapters quantify: \autoref{ch:coalgebra} makes the state minimal among
behavioral equivalents (\autoref{thm:38}), and \autoref{ch:trace} bounds
the number of iterations such a loop needs (\autoref{thm:42}).
